## What Changed in the Program

- **Quran recitation to start the session.** Every child now takes a turn reciting Quran daily before we begin. Anyone joining in between also starts by reciting (though we sometimes missed it too — may Allah help us be consistent with it).
- **Changing group.** The kids became more inconsistent, and eventually most of the older kids stopped coming while new kids started. May Allah guide us towards what is best for all the kids and for us. May Allah make this beneficial for whoever joins, and help us build a community where these kids and families learn technology and make the most of it as Muslims.
- **Building collectively instead of individually.** Everyone continues to learn, but we now make apps and games together. Previously, each child used their own AI tool to run their own prompts and build small solutions, and some ran out of free credits very quickly. Now we use a single common paid account and build upon the same two solutions, collectively building, testing and improving them (both shared below).
- **Multiple sessions per week.** Instead of a single weekly session, we now hold sessions on Friday, Saturday and Sunday, so students can join whichever suits their availability (though we had to miss multiple days due to personal engagements).
- **Combined with the science class.** Some sessions are now combined with a class on sciences taught by my wife. In the respective computer science class, we build upon a gamified tool that also helps the kids learn those concepts through visual learning (Push & Pull Lab, shared below).

## Key Takeaways

**For the students:**
- Start with the Quran. Reciting before we begin reminds us why we are learning in the first place.
- Building together is better than building alone. When everyone knows what the game should be, everyone can spot what is missing, what is broken, and what to ask for next.
- A prompt does not have to be perfect to work. Block World grew from simple, misspelled, one-line requests — one step at a time, fixing what didn't work and adding what was missing.
- What you learn in one class can become something you build in another. The forces from the science class turned into games you designed yourselves.
- Keep going with Python until the list is complete. Helping a friend through a lesson is also revising it for yourself.
- Whatever you build is by Allah's mercy, not by your own cleverness. Ask Allah to let you use it in what pleases Him.

**For the teacher:**
- One shared paid account removed the problem of free credits running out, and turned scattered individual attempts into a single project everyone owns.
- Starting from something every child already knows (a familiar game) means no time is lost explaining what to build — the children become the testers and the product owners.
- Combining with the science class gives the computer science sessions a real purpose: building tools that help learn something else, not just building for the sake of it.
- Multiple sessions across Friday, Saturday and Sunday make it easier for families to attend, but consistency still depends on routines that are actually enforced, like reciting before starting.
- Recording notes regularly matters. Writing a collective note after weeks loses detail that was obvious at the time.
- Parent engagement does not come from a single message. Most families did not follow up, and the program cannot replace what families need to begin themselves — our role is to support, not to substitute.
