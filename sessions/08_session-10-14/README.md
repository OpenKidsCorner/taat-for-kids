# Sessions 10 – 14

**Session 10:** June 27, 2026 | **Session 11:** July 11, 2026 | **Session 12:** July 18, 2026 | **Session 13:** August 01, 2026 | **Session 14:** August 08, 2026 | **Age group:** 8–12 | **Present:** 4-7

بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم

Alhamdulillah, Allah made these sessions possible. May He accept them, forgive our shortcomings, and put lasting benefit in it for the children.

## Why These Five Sessions Are Documented Together

By Allah's mercy we held all five sessions, but there is not enough distinct progress in any one of these five days to justify a write-up of its own. Honestly, these have been the least productive sessions so far, and the reason is straightforward: attendance was inconsistent throughout.

It is the summer season. Alhamdulillah, families have been able to take time out and travel to meet relatives or go out on trips, so a different subset of children showed up each time. With gaps between attendances stretching out, each session turned into re-covering ground rather than moving forward. Most of the time went into revising the same W3Schools Python activity, because whoever had missed the last day or two needed to get back to where they had been.

Allah knows what is best for every one of us. May Allah help us push harder, or take breaks, wherever each is required. May Allah's mercy help us stay on the right track and keep our vision fixed on the true and everlasting comfort, enjoyment and success — the afterlife.

So rather than five thin entries repeating the same thing, this is one honest record of a slow stretch. May Allah forgive the shortcomings in our notes.

---

## What Actually Changed

All praise is for Allah, who brought forth two real changes in this period.

### Three New Students

Alhamdulillah, three new students joined and have now attended two sessions. They are starting from the beginning — the simple robot language with **R, L, F, DL, DR** — the same activity the older students worked through in their first couple of sessions.

This means the group now runs at two very different starting points at once: newcomers on robot navigation, and the existing students somewhere along the Python path. May Allah make it easier for us to manage this versatility, and push the older kids to support those who have newly joined.

### A New Venue

By Allah's favour we have shifted to a new venue rented by the parent organization, OKC. Alhamdulillah for the space, and may Allah reward those who arranged it.

---

## Where We Fell Short

We have been inconsistent with the nafl salah, with prayers generally, and with the indoor activities that were meant to replace the outdoor games once the heat set in. None of these held steady across the five sessions.

We seek Allah's forgiveness for this. May Allah forgive our shortcomings, overlook our negligence, and by His mercy return us to consistency in what matters most — for the salah is what we will be asked about first.

---

## Progress

Alhamdulillah, one child has made the most progress by a clear margin — all of it from Allah's granting, not from our arrangement. If Allah wills, his path now splits into two tracks:

- **Programming** — moving on to LeetCode and competitive programming.
- **Web technologies** — working in parallel on web development, and supporting OKC web solution development.

May Allah bless him in it, keep his intention sincere, and make this knowledge a means of serving Him rather than a source of pride.

For the rest of the group, progress over these five sessions was limited, and the spread between the fastest and the slowest has widened. May Allah open the way for each of them at the pace that is best for them.

---

## What We Are Changing Next

Looking back over these weeks, two problems stand out:

1. **Inconsistent engagement is increasing across all students**, and it is producing an increasingly divergent set of progress levels — every child is at a different point, and the gap keeps growing.
2. **Parent engagement has not reached the level we originally planned for.**

Both of these are, at root, coordination and visibility problems. So, if Allah wills, we are prioritizing the **web app** — for collaboration, planning, and progress tracking. It should let each child's path be planned and tracked individually, make the divergence manageable rather than chaotic, and give parents a real way to stay involved.

We plan, and Allah is the best of planners. May Allah make this easy for us, put barakah in the effort, and let it serve the children and their families in a way that is pleasing to Him.

---

## Key Takeaways

**For the students:**
- Long gaps cost you real progress. Coming back after weeks away means re-covering what you already knew instead of learning something new.
- Everyone is at a different point now, and that is fine — but the way forward is your own consistency, not comparing yourself with the group.
- Welcome to the three new students, alhamdulillah. Everyone here started exactly where you are starting, with R, L, F, DL and DR.
- Whatever you learn is by Allah's mercy, not by your own cleverness. Ask Allah to make it easy for you, and ask Him to let you use it in what pleases Him.

**For the teacher:**
- Summer attendance was the single biggest constraint. Planning around it — rather than assuming a full room — would have saved several sessions from becoming pure revision.
- Running newcomers and existing students in one room now requires two parallel tracks by design, not improvisation.
- The routines that were dropped — nafl salah, indoor activity — did not fade because of a decision, but because nothing enforced them. They need to be structurally built into the session again.
- The web app is no longer a nice-to-have. Divergent individual paths and weak parent involvement are both symptoms it directly addresses, and it should be the next priority, if Allah wills.
- A slow stretch is not a failure. We do our part and leave the outcome to Allah — asking Him for consistency, and seeking His forgiveness for where we fell short.
