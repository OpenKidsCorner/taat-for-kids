These two sessions are documented together rather than separately, because the same activities continued across both days with sufficient overlap between them — the core focus on both days was self-learning Python from W3Schools, picked up where the previous day left off.

Most probably, if Allah wills, the next few sessions will be documented together too. After that, if Allah wills, we plan to switch to an app for individual and collective journey tracking. This will let us document and publish the multiple paths that each group and each individual takes.

### Wuzu and Nafl Salah

Unfortunately, on both days the nafl salah was skipped. The cause was a single change that had a knock-on effect: the teacher did not send the kids to the prayer area and instead asked them to pray in the room. With no shared space and no one visibly starting, the prayer quietly fell away — some kids did not have wudu, a couple had already prayed their nafl before coming, and most of the rest most likely skipped simply because no one else was praying.

The lesson is clear: praying together in a dedicated space is what carried the habit. Without it, the default became not praying. If Allah wills, we return to gathering everyone in the prayer area and making wudu first, so that praying — not skipping — is again the natural thing to do.

May Allah help us be consistent with the nafl salah every session, grant us better niyyah each time we stand for prayer, and accept our salah through His mercy alone.

### Revision Block

Because there was a two-week gap between Session 7 and Session 8, we repeated the same revision activity from Session 7 at the start of Session 8 to refresh everyone's memory before moving on. Running it again after the break also gave us a clear read on how much had stuck.

#### Observations

- A huge difference in understanding across the group was observed.
- Some children completed the whole activity in less than 10 minutes.
- Others took more time but were still able to complete the activity.
- A few had forgotten a couple of concepts, which we discussed and revised together.
- A couple of the younger ones stopped after solving only 2–3 problems.

### Main Activity — Self-learning Python with W3Schools

Our main focus across both days was to **self-learn Python from the [W3Schools Python tutorials](https://www.w3schools.com/python/)**.

Everyone was asked to sit in pairs — one senior kid with one junior kid — and start exploring Python, learning it together as they went.

**Session 8:** Most pairs were able to cover *Output* and *Comments*, while a couple made it through to *Data Types* and *Casting*.

**Session 9:** Everyone began by revising the Python topics from Session 8 and then continued with the same tutorials. As before, progress varied from child to child:

- Those who had only reached *Comments* last time were now able to cover up to *Data Types* and *Casting*.
- Others got through *Strings* and *Booleans*.
- One child made it all the way to *Functions*.

Midway through the session, the junior kids were grouped together separately so they could practice and revise what they had covered so far, while the elder kids were left to pace ahead based on their own skill level.

#### Observations

The difference in learning speed came down mainly to three factors:

- **Age difference** — the younger kids are slower to grasp the concepts, though this does not mean they are slow relative to their own age group.
- **Level of focus** — among the elder kids, this was the main factor; some get distracted easily.
- **Prior exposure** — the fastest learners were the ones who had already studied some of these concepts before this course.

### Breaks and Games

In a 3-hour-long continuous self-learning session, the kids often got stuck, bored, or distracted, and would try to switch over to a game. As soon as one switched to games, the others would follow. So we took a few short breaks to let everyone play computer games.

On Session 9, however, it was announced that they could only play games they had developed themselves — through AI or tools like Scratch. Those who could log into an available AI tool that still had some credits were able to prompt, create, and play their own games, while the others played their own or each other's games built in Scratch.

In future, if Allah wills, we will provide them tools to build their own team games and play together during the breaks, and also introduce some physical and board games.

### Closing

Both days ended the same way: everyone saved their progress, packed up their laptops, returned the chairs to their places, and cleaned up the room before leaving.

The standing challenge carried forward unchanged — keep working through the W3Schools Python path at home and in the next session, continuing from wherever each child had reached. The pairs were also encouraged to keep helping one another, and the faster learners to keep building small games of their own to play during the breaks. May Allah make the self-learning journey easy for each of them and keep them consistent.

## Key Takeaways

**For the students:**
- Self-learning is the real skill being built here. Working through W3Schools at your own pace — reading, trying, and helping your partner — matters more than how far you get.
- Progress is personal. Some moved quickly and some slowly, and that is fine; what counts is that each child kept moving forward from where they were.
- The games you play in the breaks should be ones you have built yourself — through AI or Scratch. Building your own fun is part of the learning.

**For the teacher:**
- Pairing a senior with a junior worked well for keeping everyone engaged, but learning speeds diverged sharply. Splitting the juniors off midway to revise at their own pace was the right call and is worth planning for from the start rather than improvising.
- A 3-hour continuous self-learning block is long for this age group; boredom and drift toward games were the main challenge. Short, structured breaks — and the rule that break-time games must be self-built — turned an inevitable distraction into something productive.
- The three drivers of pace — age, focus, and prior exposure — are now clear. Focus is the one most within reach to influence; the distractible elder kids are the group to watch and gently re-anchor.
- Continuity is working: revising the previous day's topics before continuing kept the thread intact across the two-week gap. This pattern should hold for the next few combined sessions.
- The planned move to an app for individual and collective journey tracking would make this divergence in paths a feature rather than a documentation problem — worth prioritising.
